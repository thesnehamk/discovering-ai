## Overview

Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is marked by an ongoing pattern
of inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity that interferes with functioning
or development. It is one of the most common neurodevelopmental disorders of
childhood and often continues into adulthood.

## Signs and Symptoms

Inattention symptoms include difficulty sustaining attention, not seeming to
listen when spoken to directly, difficulty following through on instructions,
disorganization, losing items needed for tasks, and being easily distracted.

Hyperactivity-impulsivity symptoms include fidgeting, difficulty remaining
seated, running or climbing in inappropriate situations, difficulty waiting for
a turn, interrupting others, and talking excessively.

Symptoms must be present in more than one setting (e.g., home and school), be
present before age 12, and be inconsistent with the person's developmental
level to support a diagnosis.

## Treatment

Current treatments focus on reducing symptoms and improving functioning:
- Behavioral therapy, including parent training in behavior management and
  classroom behavioral interventions, is recommended, particularly as a
  first-line approach for young children.
- Medication, most commonly stimulants (e.g., methylphenidate, amphetamines)
  and, for some individuals, non-stimulant options, can reduce core symptoms
  and is often combined with behavioral therapy.
- Ongoing monitoring is recommended to track symptom response, side effects,
  growth, and functioning at home and school, with treatment adjusted over
  time as needed.

A comprehensive evaluation by a qualified health care provider is needed to
diagnose ADHD and rule out other conditions that can present with similar
symptoms, such as anxiety, learning disorders, or sleep problems.
