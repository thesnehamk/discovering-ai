## Overview

Occasional anxiety is a normal part of life, but people with anxiety disorders
experience frequent, excessive worry and fear about everyday situations, and
these feelings do not go away and can worsen over time. Symptoms can interfere
with daily activities such as job performance, schoolwork, and relationships.

## Common Types

- Generalized anxiety disorder: persistent and excessive worry about a variety
  of everyday things, often accompanied by restlessness, fatigue, difficulty
  concentrating, irritability, muscle tension, and sleep disturbance.
- Panic disorder: recurrent, unexpected panic attacks — sudden periods of
  intense fear with symptoms such as a pounding heart, sweating, trembling,
  shortness of breath, and a feeling of impending doom — along with persistent
  worry about having more attacks.
- Social anxiety disorder: intense fear of social or performance situations due
  to worry about being judged, embarrassed, or rejected.
- Specific phobias: intense fear of a specific object or situation, such as
  heights or flying, that is out of proportion to the actual danger.

## Signs and Symptoms

Physical symptoms may include a racing heart, rapid breathing, sweating,
trembling, and fatigue. Behaviorally, a person may avoid places or situations
that trigger anxiety, have difficulty controlling worry, or experience
sleep problems.

## Treatment

Anxiety disorders are generally treated with psychotherapy, medication, or a
combination of both.
- Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) is a widely used and well-supported form
  of psychotherapy that helps people learn different ways of thinking about and
  reacting to anxiety-provoking situations, often including gradual, guided
  exposure to feared situations.
- Medications commonly used include certain antidepressants (particularly
  SSRIs and SNRIs) and, for short-term or situational use, anti-anxiety
  medications, prescribed and monitored by a health care provider.

People experiencing anxiety symptoms that interfere with daily life should talk
to a health care provider, who can help determine whether symptoms are due to
an anxiety disorder, another condition, or a combination.
