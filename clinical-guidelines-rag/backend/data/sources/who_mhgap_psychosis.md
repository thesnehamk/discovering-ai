## Assessment

Consider psychosis in a person presenting with any of: delusions (fixed false
beliefs not shared by others in their culture), hallucinations (hearing, seeing,
or feeling things that are not there), disorganized speech or behaviour, or
reduced ability to function in daily life. Onset may be acute or gradual.

Assess for risk factors and precipitants: recent substance use (including
stimulants and cannabis), sleep deprivation, high fever or infection, head
injury, and postpartum status. Assess for risk of harm to self or others,
including agitation and aggression, and for co-occurring depression, which
carries elevated suicide risk in this population.

Distinguish first-episode psychosis from an acute exacerbation of a known
chronic psychotic disorder, and from substance-induced or medical causes
(delirium), which require different urgent management.

## Management

Refer people with first-episode or acute psychosis to a mental health specialist
or facility whenever possible, particularly if there is significant risk, severe
agitation, or diagnostic uncertainty.

Where specialist referral is delayed or unavailable:
- Initiate a second-generation (atypical) antipsychotic at the lowest effective
  dose, titrating slowly, and monitor closely for side effects (extrapyramidal
  symptoms, metabolic changes, sedation).
- Avoid using more than one antipsychotic at a time.
- Do not use physical restraint or seclusion as a routine management strategy;
  use only as a last resort, for the shortest possible time, with continuous
  monitoring, and in line with local safeguards for human rights.

Provide psychoeducation to the person and carers about the nature of psychosis,
the treatment plan, and the importance of adherence. Engage family or carers in
ongoing support and relapse-prevention planning, including recognizing early
warning signs of relapse.

## Follow-up

Arrange regular follow-up to monitor symptom response, functioning, and
medication side effects. Encourage a gradual return to work, school, or social
activities as tolerated. Plan for long-term maintenance treatment given the high
risk of relapse after a first psychotic episode, particularly if medication is
stopped prematurely.
