## Assessment

A person presenting with depression may report at least two weeks of:
- Persistent depressed mood, most of the day, nearly every day.
- Markedly diminished interest or pleasure in activities (anhedonia).

In addition, assess for associated symptoms: disturbed sleep or appetite, feelings
of guilt or low self-worth, low energy or fatigue, poor concentration, and thoughts
of self-harm or suicide. Depression should be distinguished from normal grief or
sadness by the duration, severity, and degree of functional impairment.

Always ask directly about suicidal thoughts, plans, and access to means. Asking
about suicide does not increase risk and is an essential part of assessment.

Rule out a medical condition that could explain the symptoms (e.g. hypothyroidism,
anaemia, medication side effects) and screen for concurrent substance use or other
mental, neurological, and substance use (MNS) conditions.

## Management

First-line psychosocial interventions recommended for moderate-severe depression:
- Psychoeducation about depression for the person and, where appropriate, carers.
- Reactivation of social networks and, if possible, structured physical activity.
- Structured psychological treatment such as interpersonal therapy (IPT),
  cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT), or behavioural activation, delivered by
  trained and supervised providers.

Antidepressant medication may be offered for moderate-severe depression, especially
when psychological treatments are not available, not preferred, or have not
produced sufficient improvement. Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs)
are generally preferred as first-line agents due to their comparatively favourable
side-effect profile. Avoid combining multiple antidepressants. Continue effective
medication for at least 9–12 months after remission of symptoms to reduce relapse
risk.

Do not prescribe antidepressants for mild depression as first-line treatment;
prefer psychosocial support and follow-up.

## Follow-up and Safety Planning

Schedule regular follow-up to monitor symptoms, adherence, and side effects.
For anyone endorsing suicidal ideation, develop a collaborative safety plan,
restrict access to lethal means where feasible, involve family or carers with
the person's consent, and arrange more frequent follow-up or urgent referral if
risk appears imminent.
